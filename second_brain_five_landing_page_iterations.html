Three physical rules that kept Niklas Luhmann's 90,000 cards usable for forty years

Rule 1 — Atomic size

One card holds one argument

When a note stays small enough to read at a glance, you can rearrange an entire book outline by reordering ten cards on your desk without cutting and pasting paragraphs.

Rule 2 — Neighbour placement

File by context, not category

Instead of asking which folder a note belongs in, ask which existing note you want to see when you stumble across this one again in two years.

Rule 3 — Permanent plain text

Every card is a standard `.md` file

Your slip-box lives in a normal folder on your drive. Even if you stop using Folgezettel in 2056, your alphanumeric filenames and links remain intact in any text editor.
