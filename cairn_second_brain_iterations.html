Chapter 4 of Primary Essay in Progress: The Architecture of Recall

Why rigid hierarchies fail the working scholar

When a personal archive grows past a few hundred entries, pre-defined folders begin to conceal the very passages you need most. Filing a note under a single category forces a premature verdict on its meaning, whereas unplanned neighborhoods of rough notes invite unexpected cross-pollinationI long after the initial reading is finished.

Full-text keyword search only finds notes when your past self used the exact vocabulary you are typing today. By contrast, associative margin trails preserve the context of why two distant passages struck you as relatedII at the moment of discovery.

Over decades of fieldwork, vocabulary shifts and theories mature. Keeping dated marginalia directly alongside the original excerpt lets you audit how your own thinking evolvedIII without overwriting the historical record of your first impression.
